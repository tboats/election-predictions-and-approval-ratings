1. Executive Summary: The Post-Election Reversal

Key takeaways from our cross-sectional analysis of state approvals and economic performance.

Following President Donald Trump's comprehensive victory in the November 2024 presidential election—carrying 31 states, 312 electoral votes, and the national popular vote by +1.5%—his administration has faced an unprecedented erosion in public standing heading into the November 2026 midterms. Across the nation, public approval has fallen to between 33% and 36%, with disapproval exceeding 60%.

Core Finding: Universal Shift Below the 1:1 Parity Line
When plotting Trump's 2024 certified win margin on the x-axis against his current net approval (Approve - Disapprove) on the y-axis, 50 out of 51 jurisdictions fall directly inside the Erosion Zone below the 1:1 parity guide line. The regression line reveals a near-uniform -21.6 percentage point nationwide penalty ($y = 0.88x - 21.6$, $R^2 = 0.94$). State partisanship continues to anchor relative positioning, but the entire distribution has shifted deeply underwater.

3. Economic Segments: Who is Turning Away Fastest?

Evaluating approval erosion across 6 structural economic profiles.

Sector Breakdown & Analytical Findings:

  • Sunbelt & Consumer Growth (-21.6 pts erosion): The fastest deterioration in the nation. States like Florida (-21.1 pts), Nevada (-21.1 pts), and Georgia (-21.2 pts) were pivotal to Trump's 2024 coalition. However, acute cost-of-living spikes—including explosive home insurance rates, steep residential rents, service-sector wage stagnation, and high borrowing costs—have prompted intense buyer's remorse among suburban and independent voters.
  • Energy & Resource Extraction (-20.5 pts erosion): Trump's strongest redoubt in raw numbers (Wyoming +25%, West Virginia +19%, North Dakota +16%). Despite holding positive net ratings, these states nonetheless suffered an average 20.5-point drop from their lofty 2024 victory margins due to oil/gas price fluctuations, trade conflict spillovers, and global commodity volatility.
  • Tech, Finance & Knowledge (-20.5 pts erosion): Highly educated, service-dominant economies (CA, NY, MA, WA, CO, IL, NJ) were already hostile to Trump in 2024 (-14.3% margin) but have collapsed further to an average of -34.8% net approval, spurred by opposition to tariff schedules and federal scientific/administrative disruption.
  • Manufacturing & Industrial Heartland (-18.7 pts erosion): Industrial states (IN, WI, MI, OH, NC, SC, KY, AL) experienced an average 18.7-point erosion. Protectionist industrial rhetoric mitigated some losses, but manufacturing states heavily tied to global automotive and supply chain components (e.g., Michigan: -20% net approval) show widespread dissatisfaction with retaliatory tariffs and input costs.
  • Agriculture & Farm Belt (-18.5 pts erosion): In major agricultural commodity states (IA, NE, KS, SD, ID), retaliatory agricultural tariffs from international trading partners and elevated equipment financing costs have created visible discontent. In Iowa, Trump won by +13.7% in 2024, but his net approval is now -10%—a -23.7 point collapse.

The "Approval Tilt" Mechanism: Does Presidential Approval Shift Senate Races?

In midterms, congressional races are heavily nationalized. When a president's net approval is deeply negative (Trump at -26% net), historical data demonstrates two empirical dynamics:

  • Differential Turnout & Motivation: Base voters of the opposition party (Democrats) exhibit elevated enthusiasm, while casual presidential partisans turn out at lower rates due to economic discontent.
  • Undecided Voter Break: In close contests, undecided independent voters break 2-to-1 or 3-to-1 against the party in the White House.
  • Quantitative Shift: Accounting for state-level and national approval deficits provides an estimated +1.0 to +2.0 percentage point boost for Democratic candidates over raw pre-election polls (e.g., Maine flips to Dem +2.4%; North Carolina flips to Dem +1.6%; Georgia lean expands to Dem +5.8%; Texas tightens to GOP +0.2%).
